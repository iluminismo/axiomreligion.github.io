# Conclusion

The Duty of a Follower of AXIOM





1. "Lies" should not be its own giant section

"Lies" are not a category of existence.

They're a threat to truth.

That's why they fit naturally under:

Stewardship of Truth
Threats to Truth
2. Aesthetics deserves a place

Not because beauty is sacred, but because it relates to order, health, and signaling.

I'd put:

Hygiene
Presentation
Beauty
Architecture
Art

under either Health or Character, depending on how important you want aesthetics to be.

3. Balance is not a chapter

Balance is a meta-principle.

It should appear throughout the book.

For example:

balance between survival and comfort
balance between skepticism and certainty
balance between individual and community
4. You're missing one major thing

Time.

Many religions have an implicit time horizon of a single life.

AXIOM seems to think in generations.

I would emphasize:

long-term thinking
generational responsibility
civilization-scale planning
preserving truth beyond one's lifetime

That may become one of the most distinctive aspects of the religion.

5. Be careful with "some people don't deserve the truth"

That idea creates tension with the rest of AXIOM.

If you include it, it needs a very narrow justification such as:

self-defense,
protection against aggression,
protection of innocent people.

Otherwise readers will immediately ask:

"Who decides who deserves truth?"

and that question can undermine a religion built around truth itself.

A stronger formulation is often:

A follower of AXIOM must not knowingly deceive themselves. Deception of others may only be justified in extraordinary circumstances where it prevents greater aggression, coercion, or destruction.

That keeps the primary obligation aimed inward, which is much easier to defend philosophically and practically.
