# Introduction - What is AXIOM?

AXIOM is a religion founded upon the pursuit of self-evident truth. It is an undeniable premise that stands on its own, completely independent of human opinion or belief. You cannot argue against it, because any attempt to disprove an axiom requires using the very truth you are trying to deny.

This concept forms the foundation of the entire religion.

AXIOM begins with the conviction that reality contains self-evident truths and that the highest responsibility of a conscious being is to recognize them, understand them, and live accordingly. Truth is not treated as a preference, an interpretation, or a social construct. It is treated as something real, discoverable, and worthy of lifelong dedication.

For this reason, AXIOM is not merely a philosophy, a worldview, a political doctrine, or a personal lifestyle. It is a complete religion. It treats the pursuit of truth as a sacred commitment rather than a casual preference. Human beings experience reality through limited senses and imperfect knowledge. Complete certainty about the external world may never be attainable. Nevertheless, AXIOM maintains that reality is real, that truth exists, and that seeking it is a worthy and necessary endeavor. This commitment serves as the faith at the center of the religion. Also provides a framework for meaning, ethics, community, purpose, self-development, and the understanding of humanity's place within existence. Every aspect of the religion ultimately traces back to a single question:

What is true?

The answer is not assumed. It must be pursued continuously through reason, observation, evidence, and intellectual honesty. AXIOM does not claim that all truths have already been discovered. On the contrary, it assumes that much remains unknown. The religion is defined not by the possession of perfect knowledge, but by an uncompromising commitment to seek it.

At the foundation of AXIOM stand several self-evident truths upon which the broader religion is built.

First Truth: Life

The primary meaning of life is to survive, to adapt, to reproduce, and to pass on the best possible progeny. This is not a matter of debate it is a mathematical certainty of existence. If a species does not make survival, adaptation, and the strength of its offspring its main goal, it simply ceases to exist. Nature ruthlessly eliminates anything that ignores this rule. This biological reality also dictates our morality: we require stable families, mutual trust, and strong communities to not only survive, but thrive.

Second Truth: Knowledge

Human beings possess the unique ability to question, investigate, learn, and understand. We are not merely organisms struggling to survive; we are conscious entities capable of examining the universe and our place within it.

The search for knowledge is therefore not simply useful—it is sacred. To discover truth, expand understanding, and increase humanity's knowledge is to fulfill one of the highest functions of conscious life. To knowingly embrace falsehood, suppress truth, or abandon the pursuit of knowledge is to act against that purpose.

Third Truth: Human Action

Every individual possesses ownership over their own body, mind, labor, and property. From this follows the principle that the initiation of force, coercion, or fraud against another individual is fundamentally wrong.

AXIOM grounds this principle in the Non-Aggression Principle and the laws of Praxeology, the study of human action. Together, they provide a rational basis for understanding human behavior, cooperation, exchange, and the conditions required for a free and prosperous society.

Fourth Truth: Protection

Truth need to be defended. Knowledge can be forgotten. Facts can be distorted. History can be rewritten. Error can spread faster than understanding.

Truth must always take precedence over comfort, tradition, authority, popularity, ideology, and personal interest.

For this reason, we have a responsibility not only to seek truth, but to preserve it, protect it, refine it, and pass it to future generations.

Truth must never be accepted blindly, but neither must it be abandoned carelessly. Claims must be examined honestly, evidence must be evaluated rationally, and falsehood must be corrected when it obstructs understanding.

The pursuit of truth is incomplete without its preservation.

These principles form only the foundation of AXIOM.

They are not the entirety of the religion, nor are they intended to answer every question concerning existence, society, ethics, knowledge, culture, or human purpose. They are the bedrock upon which the larger structure is built.

The chapters that follow explore that structure in greater depth.

Everything within AXIOM begins from a simple premise: self-evident truth exists, reality matters, and the pursuit of truth is the highest calling of a conscious mind.
